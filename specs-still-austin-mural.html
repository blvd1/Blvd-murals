Spec’s x Still Austin

A site-specific hand-painted mural that turns a long commercial wall into an unmistakably Austin experience—combining brand identity, Texas imagery, native plants, architecture, music, and bold graphic scale.

ProjectSpec’s x Still Austin
LocationAustin, Texas
MediumInterior hand-painted mural
RoleDesign + mural execution
The challenge

Make the wall feel like Austin—not an ad.

The project needed to represent two recognizable brands while still feeling specific to the city around it. A traditional branded wall would have been easy to read, but easy to forget.

BLVD Murals approached the space as a complete environment instead: one panoramic composition that could read clearly from across the store, reward a closer look, and make the location feel unmistakably Austin.

The approach

Build the brand into the place.

Oversized Austin lettering anchors the composition. Around it, the mural layers recognizable local and Texas imagery into the brand story:

  • Texas Capitol architecture
  • Bluebonnets and prickly pear cactus
  • Music and guitar imagery
  • Still Austin Whiskey Co. branding
  • Spec’s visual identity
  • Austin-specific architectural references

Bold silhouettes, repeated native-flora elements, and a controlled palette keep the long wall visually cohesive while allowing each section to hold its own identity.

Commercial-scale execution

Designed around the actual space.

The mural was painted directly into the retail environment, requiring the artwork to respond to the wall dimensions, doors, ceiling lighting, architecture, sightlines, and existing conditions.

BLVD handled the work as a complete specialty-art scope—from concept development and composition through large-scale hand-painted production.

The finished wall functions as both branded artwork and a visual destination inside the store.

The result

A wall that tells people exactly where they are.

The final mural creates a memorable customer experience without relying on a conventional advertising treatment. The brands become part of a larger visual story about Austin and Texas.
